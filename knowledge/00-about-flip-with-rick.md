## Who they are [FWR site]
- **Rick Ginn** - founder. Got a corporate job out of college, started wholesaling in **2004**, quit his six-figure job that same year, and now runs one of the largest flipping/wholesaling operations in **Florida** (they work the Port Saint Lucie / Fort Pierce / Stuart area). Says he has mentored 10,000+ students.
- **Zach Ginn** - Rick's **son**. Started at 17 while working a minimum-wage bagger job; Rick told him to figure out his first deal on his own, so he learned from books and YouTube and made over **$100,000 in wholesale fees before turning 18**. He now runs the company's operations and is the main teacher in most of the YouTube videos. Wrote the free e-book "Bags to Riches" (his first 3 deals within 90 days).

## Their philosophy [FWR site]
- "We're NOT gurus. We don't charge for anything." The core course is free (FreeWholesaling.com -> home.flipwithrick.com). They contrast themselves with "$5,000 guru courses".
- Their free-course checklist: subscribe on YouTube, write down *why* you're doing this, "condition your mind that you JUST PAID ME $5,000 for this course" (take it seriously), and join the free community.
- Rick's view: wholesaling is a great way to start with little or no money, but **it can't build generational wealth on its own**. The goal is to become a "complete real estate investor": wholesaling, fix-and-flips, wholetails, rentals, creative finance (subject-to, lease options).
- Tagline for beginners (Zach's Skool group): "No money. No credit. No license. No experience."

## JV program [FWR site]
They'll partner on deals you have under contract: **50/50 split**, their dispo team sells it. Requirements: (1) must be **off-market**, (2) must be **under contract**, (3) county population **at least 50,000**. Creative-finance deals accepted.

## Student results they publish [FWR site]
Dozens of first-deal checks, mostly **$3,700-$20,000** (e.g. $4,500, $7,250, $7,500, $10,200, $13,200, $17,000), with outliers up to $30k-$76k. Useful for setting expectations: a typical first assignment fee is in the mid four to low five figures.
