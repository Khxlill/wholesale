# How wholesaling works

## What wholesaling is [General]
A wholesaler gets a property **under contract** from a motivated seller at a discount, then **assigns** (sells) that contract to a cash buyer (usually a flipper or landlord) for more than the contract price. The difference is the **assignment fee**. The wholesaler never buys the house, so it takes little money: usually just the earnest money deposit (often $100-$1,000; sometimes the title company or buyer covers it). The alternative to assigning is a **double close**: you actually buy and immediately resell in back-to-back closings, often with transactional funding, which keeps your fee private.

## Flip With Rick's 8-step process [FWR site]
1. **Find a motivated seller** (marketing).
2. **Meet with the motivated seller** (in person or virtually by phone).
3. **Get a contract signed** with the seller (acquisitions).
4. **Drop the deposit and contract off to the title company.**
5. **Find a cash buyer.**
6. **Assign (or sell) the contract for a profit** to the cash buyer (dispositions).
7. **Send the assignment of contract to the title company.**
8. **Closing day (pay day!).**

## Virtual wholesaling [FWR site + General]
Doing deals in a market you don't live in, using only a phone and computer: pull lists or Zillow leads online, cold call/text, comp on Zillow/Redfin, sign contracts by e-signature, use a local title company that handles investor deals, and find buyers online. Zach's virtual wholesaling guides (updated yearly, e.g. 2023 and 2025 editions) are among the channel's most-watched videos. For "boots on the ground" (photos, walkthroughs, lockbox access) virtual wholesalers hire local helpers or have the buyer or agent do the walkthrough [FWR video: "How to Get Boots on the Ground!"].

## Wholesaling lingo [FWR site]
- **ARV** - after repair value (what the house sells for fully fixed up)
- **MAO** - maximum allowable offer; **LAO** - least allowable offer (your opening offer)
- **EMD** - earnest money deposit
- **PSA** - purchase & sale agreement; **AOC** - assignment of contract; **LOI** - letter of intent
- **POF** - proof of funds
- **D4D** - driving for dollars
- **MLS** - multiple listing service (agent-listed homes); **DOM** - days on market
- **NOD** - notice of default; **DOT** - deed of trust; **BK** - bankruptcy
- **CFD** - cash for deed; **GWD** - general warranty deed; **AOH** - affidavit of heirship (probate/inherited)
- **TC** - title commitment
- **SFH** - single family home
- **OPM** - other people's money
- **ROI** - return on investment; **NOI** - net operating income; **CAP %** - capitalization rate
- **SEO** - search engine optimization

Extra terms [General]: **FSBO** - for sale by owner; **dispo** - dispositions (selling the deal to buyers); **buy box** - the exact kind of property a buyer wants (area, price, beds/baths, condition); **skip tracing** - finding an owner's phone/email; **comps** - comparable recent sales; **wholetail** - buy and resell with light cleanup, no full rehab; **subject-to** - buy while leaving the seller's mortgage in place; **novation** - partner with the seller to sell retail on the MLS.

## Realistic expectations [General]
- It's a sales and volume business: many calls -> a few conversations -> fewer offers -> one contract. Track your numbers (dials, conversations, offers, contracts).
- The most common beginner mistakes: offering too much (no room for a buyer), bad ARV comps, underestimating repairs, and not having buyers lined up before contracting.
- Always have an exit: an inspection/due-diligence period in your contract, and honest communication with the seller if you can't close.
