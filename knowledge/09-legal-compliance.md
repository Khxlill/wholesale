# Is wholesaling legal? Laws and compliance

**Not legal advice.** Flip With Rick's own page says: "Please read laws & advice with lawyers & title companies before wholesaling" [FWR site]. Laws change often; confirm current rules for your state with a local real estate attorney or title company.

## State wholesaling laws Flip With Rick lists [FWR site, as of 2026 page]
Current laws: **South Carolina** (2023-24 H.4754), **Oregon** (HB 4058, 2024), **Iowa** (HF 2394), **Virginia** (HB 917, 2024).
Others: **Nebraska** (LB 892), **Oklahoma** (HB 3104), **Arizona** (2022 law), **Illinois** (SB 1872, 2023), **Kentucky** (HB 62).

Common themes in these laws [General]: you must **disclose in writing** to the seller that you're a wholesaler who intends to assign or resell (and sometimes that the seller may not get market value); some states require a **real estate license** for repeated wholesaling or for marketing a property you don't own; some give the seller a **right to cancel** within a set number of days; and many prohibit **advertising the property itself** without a license, so you market your **contract/equitable interest** instead. Illinois, for example, treats wholesaling more than once in 12 months as licensed activity. Check the current text for your state.

Flip With Rick covers this in "Wholesaling Laws" (Day 23 of the challenge) and "Is Wholesaling Real Estate Legal?".

## Calling and texting rules [FWR video: "Will You Get Sued For Texting AND Cold Calling Wholesaling Real Estate"; General]
- The federal **TCPA** and **National Do Not Call Registry** apply to calls and texts. Scrub lists against federal and state DNC lists; honor opt-outs immediately ("stop", "remove me"); call only during allowed hours (generally 8am-9pm local time; some states are stricter).
- Mass **texting** and auto-dialed or pre-recorded calls to cell phones carry the most legal risk (TCPA statutory damages per message). Many SMS platforms now require registration and consent; follow their compliance rules.
- Calling a FSBO seller about the house they've publicly listed is lower-risk than blasting a purchased list, but DNC and state rules can still apply.
- Some states have their own telemarketing registration or "mini-TCPA" laws (e.g. Florida, Oklahoma).

## Honest dealing [General]
- Tell sellers you're an investor and may assign or resell.
- Don't claim to be an agent or to "list" their house unless licensed.
- Only sign contracts you intend to close or assign, and use your inspection period honestly.
- Keep earnest money with a title company or attorney, never informally.
- Watch for **equity-stripping/foreclosure-rescue** rules when dealing with sellers in default. Several states regulate those deals heavily.
