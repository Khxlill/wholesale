## The two documents [General]
1. **Purchase & Sale Agreement (PSA)** between you and the seller. Key items:
   - **Buyer name**: "[Your name or LLC] **and/or assigns**" so the contract is assignable (or an explicit assignment clause).
   - **Price**, **earnest money deposit (EMD)** amount and when it's due, held by the title company.
   - **Inspection / due-diligence period** (e.g. 7-21 days) during which you can cancel. This protects you if repairs are worse or you can't find a buyer. Be upfront with the seller about it.
   - **Closing date** (often 21-45 days out, enough time to find a buyer).
   - **Access** for you and your buyers/contractors to walk through.
   - **As-is** condition, who pays closing costs, title company named.
   - Some states require specific wholesaler disclosures in or alongside the contract (see legal file).
2. **Assignment of Contract (AOC)** between you and your cash buyer. Transfers your rights under the PSA for an **assignment fee**, usually with a **non-refundable deposit** from the buyer (commonly $5k-$10k or a % of the price) so they don't walk.

## Step 4 of their process: drop the contract and deposit at title [FWR site]
Right after signing, send the signed PSA to the title company and pay the EMD. Title opens the file, orders the **title search**, and issues a **title commitment (TC)** showing liens, mortgages, judgments, probate issues, or code violations that must be cleared.

## Wholesale-friendly title companies [General]
Not every title company or closing attorney handles assignments or double closings. Call and ask: "Do you close **assignments**? Do you do **double closings** and accept **transactional funding**? How do you show the assignment fee on the settlement statement?" Ask local investors and cash buyers which title company they use. In attorney-closing states, you'll work with a real estate attorney instead.

## After the contract is signed [FWR video topic; General checklist]
1. Send contract + EMD to title; confirm they opened the file.
2. Get photos/video and access (lockbox or seller schedule).
3. Re-check ARV and repairs; update your MAO and buyer price.
4. Market to your cash buyers (see dispo file) and schedule walkthroughs.
5. Sign the **assignment** with the buyer, collect their non-refundable deposit, send the AOC to title.
6. Keep the seller updated weekly; handle title issues early.
7. Close: title pays off the seller and pays your **assignment fee** on the settlement statement. **Pay day.**

## Double closing [General]
Instead of assigning, you buy the property (A-B closing) and immediately resell it (B-C closing), usually the same day, often with **transactional funding** (a short-term lender that funds your purchase for a fee, often 1-2%). Use it when the contract can't be assigned (common with MLS/bank-owned listings), when the spread is large and you don't want it visible, or when a buyer won't accept a big assignment fee. It costs more (two sets of closing costs plus funding fees).

## Proof of funds (POF) [FWR video topic; General]
Sellers and agents may ask for it. Options: a POF letter from a transactional or hard-money lender, or a cash buyer/partner's POF used with permission. Never fake a POF.
