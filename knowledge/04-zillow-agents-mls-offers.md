## Why on-market deals work [General]
- Thousands of listings, free to search, and the agent's phone number is on every listing.
- Stale listings (high days on market, multiple price cuts, vacant, needs work) have sellers whose expectations are coming down.
- The agent only gets paid if it sells. A clean cash offer that closes is attractive even if it's low.
- Volume game: send many offers; a small % gets accepted or countered.

## Finding the right listings on Zillow [General]
Filter for: **days on Zillow 60-90+**, price cuts, keywords ("as-is", "cash only", "investor special", "TLC", "handyman", "fixer", "estate sale", "needs work"), and property types your buyers want. Skip new construction and fully renovated homes. Prioritize houses that clearly need work, where a cash buyer is the natural buyer.

## The agent call [General structure]
1. "Hi, is this [agent]? I'm calling about [address] on Zillow. Is it still available?"
2. Get info: Why is the seller selling? Is it vacant? Any offers? Have there been price drops? What does it need? Is the seller flexible on price?
3. Explain you're a **cash buyer**: as-is, no inspections contingencies beyond a short due-diligence period, close in 2-3 weeks (or on their timeline).
4. Give a number (your LAO/MAO from the deal calculator) and **explain it with the repairs**: "Comps fixed up are around $X; it needs about $Y in work; I'm at $Z cash."
5. Ask: "Would you be willing to present that to your seller?" Agents must present written offers, so **send it in writing** (email or their offer system) with **proof of funds** [FWR video: "How to Get a Proof of Funds Letter in 60 Seconds"].
6. Follow up on every "no" every 1-2 weeks. Price cuts happen.

Keep it respectful: agents talk to each other. Being clear, fast, and closing what you sign gets you repeat deals and pocket listings from those agents [FWR video: "How to Get FREE Wholesaling DEALS from Real Estate Agents"].

## Contracts and assignment on listed properties [General - check state rules]
- Listing agents usually use their state association's purchase contract. Ask whether the seller allows **assignment** ("buyer and/or assigns"); many listing agreements or sellers don't. If not, plan a **double close** (buy with transactional funding, resell same day).
- Disclose that you're an investor who may assign or resell. Several states now require written disclosure (see legal file).
- Use an **inspection / due-diligence period** so you can confirm repairs and line up a buyer.
- Earnest money on MLS deals is often larger. Know when it becomes non-refundable.
- Don't market the agent's listing as your own; market your **contract position** where state law allows, and follow MLS rules.

## Off-market vs on-market [General]
Off-market (FSBO calls, lists, D4D, texting) usually gives bigger spreads but takes more marketing. On-market (agents) is free and fast to start but spreads are thinner and competition higher. Flip With Rick teaches both; the JV program only accepts **off-market** deals [FWR site].
