# Talking to sellers: the 4 pillars, scripts, objections

## The 4 pillars / questions you must ask [FWR site]
1. **Motivation** - why are they interested in selling the house?
2. **Condition** - how much repair work does it need? (roof, pool, A/C, kitchen updates, etc.)
3. **Timeframe** - when do they need to sell? (two weeks, one month, six months...)
4. **Price** - how much do they want, and **need**, to sell it for?

## Zillow FSBO call - sample script built on the 4 pillars [General]
> **Intro:** "Hi, is this [name]? This is [you], I'm a local investor. I saw your house on [street] listed for sale by owner on Zillow. Is it still available?"
>
> **Permission:** "I buy houses for cash, as-is, and I can close fast. Mind if I ask a few quick questions to see if it's a fit?"
>
> **Motivation:** "What has you thinking about selling?" ... "How long have you been trying to sell?" ... "If it sold, what's next for you?"
>
> **Condition:** "Tell me about the condition. How old is the roof? A/C? Anything with the foundation, plumbing, or electrical? When were the kitchen and baths last updated? Anything I'd need to fix right away?"
>
> **Timeframe:** "If we agreed on a price, how soon would you want to close? Is there a date you need to be out by?"
>
> **Price:** "I see you're listed at $X. Is there any flexibility for a cash, as-is buyer who covers closing costs and closes on your timeline?" ... "What's the lowest you'd take if I could close in [14] days?" (Let silence work.)
>
> **Next step:** either give your offer (opening at your LAO) or: "Let me run the numbers on the repairs and comps and call you back by [time]."

Getting the price out of them [General]: ask "what are you hoping to get?", then "is that flexible for cash and a quick close?", then "what's the lowest you'd consider?". If they won't name a number, give a range ("most houses that need this much work sell to investors for somewhere between $A and $B; would that range work?") and watch the reaction.

## Making a low offer without offending [FWR video topic: "How To Make A Low-Ball Offer (and NOT Piss-Off the Seller)"; General technique]
- Earn it first: ask the 4 pillars before talking numbers.
- **Show your math**: "Fixed up, homes like yours sell for about $ARV. It needs about $repairs. Investors also have to cover holding, closing and selling costs and make a profit, so the most I can do is $offer."
- Sell the **benefits** that matter to them: cash, as-is, no repairs, no showings, no commissions, no closing costs, close on their date, take what they want and leave the rest.
- Ask permission: "I'm going to give you a number. It's probably lower than you'd like. Is that OK?"
- **Never argue**; leave the door open: "I understand. If anything changes, can I check back in a couple of weeks?"

## Common objections [General]
- **"That's way too low."** -> "I understand. What number would make sense for you?" / Walk through the repair and ARV math again. / "Is it the price, or is there something else that matters more, like timing?"
- **"Zillow says it's worth more."** -> "Zillow's estimate assumes it's fixed up and listed with an agent. That's the after-repair price. I'm buying it as-is for cash, and I take on the repairs and risk."
- **"I'll just list it with an agent."** -> "That might get you more. After 6% commission, repairs, closing costs, and months of payments, what would you net? My offer is what you net, with no fees."
- **"I need to think about it / talk to my spouse."** -> "Of course. What part do you want to think through? When can we all talk?"
- **"Are you a realtor? Are you going to flip this to someone else?"** -> Be honest: "I'm an investor. I might fix it myself, partner with another investor, or assign my contract to one. Either way, you get the price we agree on." (Honesty here is also required by law in a growing number of states.)
- **"Why would you get a fee?"** -> "I'm doing the work: finding the buyer, covering the deposit, handling title. My price to you is fixed, and you pay no commission."

## When the seller wants to back out [FWR site: "What to Do If SELLER Wants to BACK OUT of Contract?"; General]
Find out why (cold feet, family pressure, a higher offer, moving plans changed). Re-sell the benefits, solve the actual problem, and remember they signed a contract. But a forced, unhappy closing is rarely worth it. Don't threaten. If you can't save it, let it go professionally and keep the relationship.
