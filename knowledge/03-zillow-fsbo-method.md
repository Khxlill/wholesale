## Why Zillow FSBOs [General]
- **Free**: no list or skip-tracing cost. The owner's phone number is usually right on the listing.
- **Already want to sell**: you're not convincing them to sell, only on price/terms/speed.
- **No agent in the middle** and no commission to protect, so a cash, as-is, close-fast offer can beat their alternatives.
- FSBO sellers who've sat on the market a while are often frustrated and flexible.

## Building the list [General method; Zillow menus change over time]
1. Pick a market (see lead-sources file: county of 50k+ people, lots of older homes and investor activity).
2. On Zillow, search the city/county, **For Sale**.
3. **Listing type** filter -> choose **"By owner & other"** and keep only **owner-posted** (uncheck agent-listed, new construction, foreclosures, auctions).
4. Optional filters: **Days on Zillow** (30+ or 90+ days = more motivated), **price cut**, **keywords** like "as-is", "investor", "cash only", "handyman", "TLC", "fixer", "needs work", "estate", "motivated", "must sell".
5. Look at photos and description for distress: dated kitchen/baths, damage, vacant, "bring your contractor".
6. Copy into a spreadsheet or CRM (Flip With Rick uses Google Sheets, Podio, and XLeads CRM [FWR site]): address, asking price, beds/baths/sq ft, days listed, price history, phone, notes, quick ARV guess.
7. Fresh FSBOs appear daily. Re-check new listings every day ("sort by newest") and call them first.

## Calling FSBO sellers [FWR site pillars + General]
Goal of the first call: **qualify with the 4 pillars** (Motivation, Condition, Timeframe, Price; see scripts file), build rapport, and either make an offer or set an appointment/follow-up.

Tips:
- Lead with honesty: you're a local investor who buys houses for cash, as-is, and can close quickly; you're not an agent and don't charge commission.
- Ask why they're selling before talking price. Motivation (relocation, tired landlord, inherited, divorce, behind on payments, repairs they can't afford) decides everything.
- Their asking price is usually retail. Your job is to find out what they **need**, not what they want: "If I could close in two weeks with cash and you didn't have to fix anything, what's the lowest you'd take?"
- Expect most to say no. FSBOs who've listed for 60-90+ days, who have price cuts, or whose house needs work are your best prospects. **Follow up**: many deals happen on the 3rd-10th touch, after the seller's realtor-free plan stalls.
- Respect do-not-call requests and calling-hour rules (see legal file).

## Tired landlords from Zillow rentals [FWR video: "How to Pull FREE Tired Landlord Lists from Zillow"]
Rental listings posted **by owner** (not property managers) are landlords dealing with vacancies and tenants themselves. Search Zillow **Rentals**, filter to owner-listed, look for long-listed or repeatedly listed units, and call: "I saw your rental on Zillow. Have you ever thought about just selling it instead of dealing with tenants?" [General phrasing].

## Finding cash buyers on Zillow [FWR video: "The NEW Way Of Finding Cash Buyers on Zillow For Wholesaling With $0"]
General method: look at **recently sold** homes in your target zip codes for (a) houses that sold cheap and were re-sold renovated a few months later (that's a flipper), and (b) homes that sold and are now listed **for rent** (that's a landlord). Look up who bought them in county records (often an LLC), skip-trace the owner, and add them to your buyers list. Also note which **listing agents** keep selling flips; they represent investors.
